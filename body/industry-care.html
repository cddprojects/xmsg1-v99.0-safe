Care and community jobs in Singapore: steady resident demand in late 2026

Health and social services remained one of the main sources of resident employment growth, with full-time and part-time shift work at the centre.

WNOpty Quest Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min readOutlook: Steady

Work types most often seen: full-time and part-time shift work

Where the sector stands

In the third quarter of 2026, resident employment growth was led by public administration and education, transportation and storage, and health and social services. Care sits in that last group, which covers hospitals, clinics, eldercare, disability support, and community services.

The sector was not among those driving the rise in retrenchments in the second quarter; those increases were concentrated in manufacturing, information and communications, and financial services. In June, entry-level PMET vacancies — roles with an advertised minimum salary between $2,300 and $5,000 — spanned sectors that included health and social services.

Nursing, therapy, pharmacy, and social work are regulated professions with registration requirements. Support and administrative roles more often turn on shift patterns, language, and the centre where the work takes place.

How roles are usually advertised

Care advertisements typically state the workplace, the shift pattern, and whether the role is employment or a freelance engagement. Part-time care work under a contract of service is employment, with CPF applying for citizens and permanent residents once monthly wages exceed $50.

For regulated roles, the meaning of "registered" or "enrolled" is set by the relevant professional board. Descriptions commonly mention shift attendance, record-keeping, and work with families.

Details commonly found in postings

  • Registration or enrolment requirements for regulated roles.
  • Shift patterns, including nights and weekends, and how far ahead rosters are published.
  • Whether the role is employment or freelance, and who pays CPF.
  • The centre or centres where the work is based.

Sources

  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Advance Release, third quarter 2026 (30 October 2026)
  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Report, second quarter 2026 (21 September 2026)
