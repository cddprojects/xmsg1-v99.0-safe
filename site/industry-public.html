Industry guide · Public and essential services

This is where much of the resident employment growth sat in the third quarter.

Supporting resident employment

Updated 19 November 2026The next official look at sectors and vacancies is the MOM Labour Market Report for the third quarter of 2026, expected in mid-December 2026.

What the market looks like

MOM's third-quarter advance release said resident employment growth continued to be supported mainly by essential and public services: public administration and education, transportation and storage, and health and social services. This page covers public administration and education. Care and transport have their own pages because the day-to-day work is different.

Hiring here often follows budget cycles, schemes, and fixed windows, rather than a rolling private-sector advertisement. A quiet week on a job portal can simply be the gap between those windows.

The rise in retrenchments in the second quarter was concentrated in other sectors. Public administration and education were not among them. One agency can still differ from the sector as a whole.

What the advertisements tend to say

Scheme details are usually on the agency's own site or on Careers@Gov, as well as on any repost. Citizenship conditions, medical or security checks, and closing dates are commonly part of those pages.

An education role can ask for a qualification, a registration, or both. Early-childhood and school roles are different schemes. The agency's eligibility page explains what a certificate is for. Mid-career schemes sometimes recognise work experience and sometimes use a trainee pay point. The advertisement is where you can see which of those applies.

Worth looking for in an advertisement

  • The closing date, and whether late applications are accepted.
  • Any citizenship or residency conditions.
  • Registration, a medical check, or security clearance, where the scheme includes them.
  • The pay point for mid-career entrants, if the scheme publishes one.

How to read the figures

  • They describe the country as a whole. One employer can look quite different.
  • Hours, the workplace, and the type of contract are written in each advertisement.
  • A fee just to be considered is not part of ordinary hiring.
