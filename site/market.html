Market update

The job market is growing, and it is uneven.

Total employment rose again in the third quarter of 2026. Resident hiring was concentrated in essential and public services. Outward-looking sectors were quieter, and retrenchments stayed elevated there.

Updated 19 November 2026Next official sector and vacancy release: MOM Labour Market Report 3Q 2026, expected mid-December 2026.

What to take from this

  • Do not read total employment growth as resident job growth.
  • Care, transport and storage, and public services are where resident employment is still being supported.
  • Technology, finance, and professional services had subdued resident growth in the third quarter.
  • Vacancy counts below are from June 2026. Newer vacancy figures are not out yet.

Employment

Total employment, excluding migrant domestic workers, grew by 27,700 in the third quarter of 2026. That was faster than the 11,400 increase in the second quarter and the 25,100 increase a year earlier. Both resident and non-resident employment grew faster than in the previous quarter.

Resident employment growth was mainly in public administration and education, transportation and storage, and health and social services. Resident growth in information and communications, financial services, and professional services was subdued. Non-resident employment made up most of the total increase, mainly in construction and manufacturing.

Unemployment and retrenchment

Overall unemployment stayed at 2.0% in September 2026. Resident and citizen rates edged up. In the June quarter, the resident long-term unemployment rate was 1.0%, from 0.9% in March. The advance release does not update that long-term rate.

Retrenchments increased from 4,620 in the second quarter to 4,800 in the third. MOM said they remained elevated in selected outward-oriented sectors, and that business reorganisation was still the main reason. In the second quarter, the increases were concentrated in manufacturing (670 to 870), information and communications (530 to 720), and financial services (560 to 710). Reorganisation or restructuring accounted for 72.1% of second-quarter retrenchments.

Among residents retrenched in the second quarter, 54.9% were back in employment within six months, down from 60.7% in the first quarter. The twelve-month re-entry rate was 69.8%. A lower six-month rate means a search can take longer even when unemployment looks low. It is not a forecast for any one person.

Vacancies, as of June

Job vacancies fell from 73,300 in March 2026 to 68,600 in June, and from 76,200 in June 2025. The vacancy rate was 2.8%. The largest declines were in financial services (5,800 to 4,500) and information and communications (5,300 to 4,400).

Entry-level PMET vacancies were 31,700 in June, 45.3% of all vacancies. MOM describes these as professional, manager, executive, and technician vacancies advertised with a minimum salary between $2,300 and $5,000, and notes that they spanned sectors such as health and social services and professional services. That range is a statistical definition, not a recommended wage.

What employers said they might do next

In September 2026, 42.3% of firms expected to hire in the next three months, up from 30.5% in August. The share expecting to raise wages rose from 16.9% to 19.9%. The share expecting to retrench rose from 2.8% to 3.1%. MOM said hiring and wage expectations improved in September but stayed below July's levels, and that firms remained selective.

The third-quarter Labour Market Report, expected in mid-December 2026, will add sector employment splits, vacancy counts, turnover, and re-entry rates. Until then, treat vacancy numbers on this site as June figures.
