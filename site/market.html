Singapore's job market in the third quarter of 2026: more jobs, unevenly spread

Employment grew faster than in the spring, unemployment held at 2.0%, and hiring intentions recovered. Underneath, resident hiring was concentrated in a handful of sectors.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 3 min read

On the surface, the third quarter of 2026 was a good one for Singapore's labour market. Total employment, excluding migrant domestic workers, grew by 27,700 — well ahead of the 11,400 recorded in the second quarter and above the 25,100 of a year earlier. Unemployment was unchanged at 2.0% in September.

The detail tells a more nuanced story. Most of the increase was non-resident employment, mainly in construction and manufacturing. Resident employment also grew faster than in the previous quarter, but that growth was concentrated in a narrow band of sectors.

Where resident hiring grew

The Ministry of Manpower's advance release identified public administration and education, transportation and storage, and health and social services as the main sources of resident employment growth. These are sectors where full-time and shift-based part-time roles make up much of the hiring.

By contrast, resident employment growth in information and communications, financial services, and professional services was subdued. These outward-oriented sectors are also where hybrid, online, contract, and freelance arrangements are most commonly advertised, which is why the work types series on this blog reads each arrangement against the sector it usually sits in.

A rising national total and a quiet resident market in technology or finance can both be true in the same quarter.

Unemployment and retrenchment

While the overall rate held at 2.0%, the resident unemployment rate edged up from 2.9% in August to 3.0% in September, and the citizen rate from 3.0% to 3.1%. Retrenchments rose from 4,620 in the second quarter to 4,800 in the third, though the incidence was unchanged at 2.0 per 1,000 employees. MOM said retrenchments remained elevated in selected outward-oriented sectors, with business reorganisation still the main reason.

The second-quarter report gives a clearer picture of where those job losses were. Retrenchments rose in manufacturing (from 670 to 870), information and communications (530 to 720), and financial services (560 to 710). Reorganisation or restructuring accounted for 72.1% of the total.

Re-entry has also slowed. Of residents retrenched in the second quarter, 54.9% were back in work within six months, down from 60.7% in the first quarter. The twelve-month figure was 69.8%. The resident long-term unemployment rate was 1.0% in June, up from 0.9% in March.

Vacancies, as of June

Job vacancies fell to 68,600 in June 2026, from 73,300 in March and 76,200 a year earlier, putting the vacancy rate at 2.8% and the ratio at 1.48 vacancies for every unemployed person. The sharpest declines were in financial services (5,800 to 4,500) and information and communications (5,300 to 4,400).

Entry-level PMET vacancies — professional, manager, executive, and technician roles advertised with a minimum salary between $2,300 and $5,000 — numbered 31,700, or 45.3% of the total. That salary band is the survey's definition of the group, not a benchmark for pay.

What employers expect next

Hiring sentiment improved in September. The share of firms expecting to hire over the next three months rose to 42.3% from 30.5% in August, and the share expecting to raise wages rose to 19.9% from 16.9%. The share expecting to retrench also edged up, from 2.8% to 3.1%. MOM noted that expectations remained below July's levels and that firms were staying selective.

What to watch in December

The full Labour Market Report for the third quarter, expected in mid-December 2026, will add sector-level employment, updated vacancy counts, turnover, and re-entry rates. Until then, the vacancy figures cited across this blog remain those for June.
