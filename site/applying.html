What a genuine job advertisement in Singapore usually includes

Whether the role is full-time, part-time, hybrid, online, contract, or freelance, legitimate postings share a common set of details. Some requests never belong in ordinary hiring.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min read

Job advertisements in Singapore vary widely in length and style, but legitimate ones tend to share a core set of details. Knowing what those are makes it easier to compare roles — and to recognise the requests that do not belong in ordinary hiring.

The market behind the advertisement

The same application can meet very different conditions depending on the sector. In technology and finance, where vacancies fell and restructuring has been common, advertisements tend to be specific about systems and processes. In care and public services, where resident employment is still growing, they more often mention shifts, registration, or scheme windows. The industry reports on this blog cover each sector in more detail.

What a legitimate posting usually contains

  • The legal name of the employer, not only a brand or a messaging-app group.
  • Whether the role is employment or freelance, along with the hours, workplace, and pay basis.
  • A closing date where relevant, and the documents requested.
  • No charge to apply, interview, or receive an offer.

Where applications are usually sent

Most roles are listed on the employer's own careers page. MyCareersFuture, the government portal run by SWDA with GovTech, can be browsed without logging in; applying through it uses Singpass and is open to citizens and permanent residents. Reposts on other job boards can help with discovery, but the employer's own listing is the authoritative version.

What a first application typically includes

A first application is usually a CV and a short cover note. Personal identifiers — NRIC numbers, passport details, bank details, and dates of birth — are rarely needed at this stage. Employers who need a photograph generally say so in the advertisement.

Ordinary employers do not charge candidates to apply, to interview, or to receive an offer.

Requests that fall outside ordinary hiring

  • A payment, a starter kit that must be purchased, or a cheque to be banked and partly returned.
  • A Singpass login, a one-time password, or a photograph of an NRIC in an early conversation.
  • Bank details requested before a written offer from an identifiable employer.
  • An interview conducted only through a personal messaging app, with no company email or published phone number.

Suspected scams can be reported to the ScamShield helpline on 1799, and losses to the Singapore Police Force. ACRA's business-profile search shows whether a company is registered, though registration alone does not confirm that a job offer is genuine.
