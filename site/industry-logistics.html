Industry guide · Logistics and industry

Transport supported resident jobs. Manufacturing's growth was mostly non-resident.

Split between resident transport jobs and industrial hiring

Updated 19 November 2026The next official look at sectors and vacancies is the MOM Labour Market Report for the third quarter of 2026, expected in mid-December 2026.

What the market looks like

Transportation and storage was one of the sectors supporting resident employment growth in the third quarter of 2026, together with public administration, education, and health and social services. That includes land transport, warehousing, and parts of the supply chain, not every job with "logistics" in the title.

Manufacturing is a different picture. Non-resident employment drove most of the economy's employment increase in the third quarter, mainly in construction and manufacturing. In the second quarter, manufacturing retrenchments rose from 670 to 870. Construction's employment growth is largely work-permit hiring. A large total-employment number there is not a count of resident vacancies.

June vacancy figures for these sectors will be refreshed in the mid-December report. Until then, the employment and retrenchment split above is the later evidence.

What the advertisements tend to say

In transport and warehousing, advertisements often turn on licence class, shift, and site. In manufacturing, a production-operator role and a technician or engineer role are different advertisements. Retrenchment has been higher here than in care or public services, so the length of a contract is worth reading carefully.

Safety orientation and site access are part of the job. If someone asks you to pay for a "site pass" before a named employer has confirmed the role in writing, that is not how ordinary hiring works. A genuine role also appears on the employer's own website or a known public portal.

Worth looking for in an advertisement

  • The licence or certificate named in the advertisement.
  • Shift hours, how overtime usually works, and the work site.
  • Whether the role is employment, daily-rated work, or freelance.
  • Who issues any pass or permit, and whether a fee is involved.

National figures come from MOM's Labour Market Advance Release for the third quarter of 2026 and the Labour Market Report for the second quarter of 2026.

How to read the figures

  • A fee just to be considered is not part of ordinary hiring.
