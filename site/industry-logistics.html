Split between resident transport jobs and industrial hiring

Updated 19 November 2026Next official sector and vacancy release: MOM Labour Market Report 3Q 2026, expected mid-December 2026.

The situation

Transportation and storage was one of the sectors supporting resident employment growth in the third quarter of 2026, together with public administration, education, and health and social services. That includes land transport, warehousing, and parts of the supply chain, not every job with "logistics" in the title.

Manufacturing is a different picture. Non-resident employment drove most of the economy's employment increase in the third quarter, mainly in construction and manufacturing. In the second quarter, manufacturing retrenchments rose from 670 to 870. Construction's employment growth is largely work-permit hiring. A large total-employment number there is not a count of resident vacancies.

June vacancy figures for these sectors will be refreshed in the mid-December report. Until then, use the employment and retrenchment split above rather than an older vacancy chart.

If you are applying

Advertisements in transport and warehousing often turn on licence class, shift, and site. Name the licence you hold and the shifts you can work. Do not imply a licence you plan to get later.

In manufacturing, separate production-operator roles from technician and engineer roles. Retrenchment has been higher in this sector than in care or public services, so ask whether the role replaces a leaver or sits in a new line, and how long the contract runs.

Safety orientation and site access rules are part of the job, not an extra. If someone asks you to pay for a "site pass" before any employer has confirmed the role in writing, stop and check the employer's own channel.

What to check in the advertisement

  • Licence or certificate named in the ad, and whether you already hold it.
  • Shift hours, overtime practice, and the work site.
  • Employment versus a daily-rated or freelance arrangement.
  • Who issues any pass or permit, and whether you are being asked to pay for it.
