Full-time work in Singapore: where hiring is holding at the end of 2026

Full-time employment remains the backbone of resident jobs. This year, which sectors are doing the hiring matters as much as how much hiring there is.

WNWorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min read

Full-time work is still the dominant pattern in Singapore's resident workforce. What changed over 2026 is where that full-time hiring is happening. The latest Ministry of Manpower figures show steady demand in essential and public services, and a markedly more selective market in the sectors that drove much of the hiring in earlier years.

Where full-time hiring is holding

Resident employment growth in the third quarter of 2026 was led by public administration and education, transportation and storage, and health and social services. Many of the roles in these sectors are full-time positions, often built around rosters, schemes, or fixed recruitment windows rather than rolling advertisements.

That pattern matters for anyone reading the headline employment number. The 27,700 increase in total employment was mostly non-resident, concentrated in construction and manufacturing. The resident story sits in the sectors above.

Where it has become more selective

In financial services, information and communications, and professional services, resident employment growth was subdued in the third quarter. Vacancies had already fallen by June: from 5,800 to 4,500 in financial services, and from 5,300 to 4,400 in information and communications. Retrenchments in both sectors rose in the second quarter, with business reorganisation the main driver.

Full-time roles in these sectors have not disappeared, but the advertisements that remain tend to be more specific — naming a system, a regulation, or a measurable piece of work rather than a broad job family.

Full-time work is still where most resident hiring happens. The difference this year is which sectors are doing it.

How a full-time role is usually structured

A full-time position is a contract of service, typically built around a standard working week. The Employment Act sets the baseline for most employees, covering working hours, overtime, rest days, and leave. Probation, notice periods, and benefits are set out in the contract itself. For Singapore citizens and permanent residents, the employer contributes to CPF on wages above $50 a month, alongside the employee's share once wages pass $500.

What full-time advertisements commonly state

  • The legal name of the employer and the work location.
  • Working hours, including shifts, weekends, or on-call arrangements where relevant.
  • The salary basis and, increasingly, a salary range.
  • Any registration, licence, or qualification the role depends on.

The outlook

In MOM's September poll, 42.3% of firms said they expected to hire in the next three months, up from 30.5% in August, while remaining selective. The mid-December labour market report will show whether that improvement reached the sectors where full-time hiring has been quietest.

Sources

  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Advance Release, third quarter 2026 (30 October 2026)
  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Report, second quarter 2026 (21 September 2026)
