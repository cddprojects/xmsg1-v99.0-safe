Technology jobs in Singapore: hiring continues, on tighter terms

Information and communications vacancies fell by June and resident growth slowed in the third quarter. Hybrid, online, contract, and freelance roles remain part of the mix.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min readOutlook: Cautious

Work types most often seen: hybrid, online, contract, and freelance

Where the sector stands

Resident employment growth in information and communications was subdued in the third quarter of 2026, alongside financial services and professional services. That marks a change from the second quarter, when the sector still recorded resident employment gains.

Vacancies fell from 5,300 in March to 4,400 in June, and retrenchments rose from 530 in the first quarter to 720 in the second. MOM linked higher recruitment and resignation rates in the sector to business restructuring and movement between firms rather than to broad-based expansion.

Advertisements for data, cloud, cybersecurity, and applied AI work continue to appear. In a restructuring year, they tend to describe a tool in the context of day-to-day work rather than a list of product names.

How roles are usually advertised

Openings are fewer than a year ago, and the advertisements that remain tend to name a system, the scale of the workload, and the outcome expected. Hybrid arrangements are common, and some roles are advertised as remote or on fixed-term contracts, though many technology roles remain office-based.

On 16 September 2026, the Skills and Workforce Development Agency published a public catalogue of more than 200 AI-related courses, with a time-limited tool subscription for eligible citizens who enrol and attend. It is a list of courses, not of jobs.

Details commonly found in postings

  • The system or product the role works on.
  • Whether the role is permanent, fixed-term, or a vendor placement, and who the employer of record is.
  • Office days, time-zone overlap, and on-call expectations.
  • No fees for equipment, starter kits, or training as a condition of being considered.

Sources

  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Advance Release, third quarter 2026 (30 October 2026)
  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Report, second quarter 2026 (21 September 2026)
