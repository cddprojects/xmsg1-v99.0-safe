Online work in Singapore: how remote roles are being advertised in 2026

There is no official count of online jobs in Singapore. The closest guide is the sector data — and the warnings about online job scams.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min read

"Online work" covers a wide range of arrangements: fully remote employment with a Singapore company, contract roles for regional teams, and freelance projects delivered entirely over the internet. It is one of the most searched-for kinds of work, and one of the least clearly defined.

No separate official count

The Ministry of Manpower does not publish a count of online or remote jobs. The nearest indicator is the data for the sectors where such roles are most common. In information and communications, vacancies fell from 5,300 in March to 4,400 in June 2026, and retrenchments rose from 530 in the first quarter to 720 in the second. Resident employment growth in the sector was subdued in the third quarter.

Online roles continue to be advertised within that tighter market, mainly in software, data, design, and parts of professional services.

Online, remote, and hybrid are not the same

Many advertisements that describe a role as online still include overlap hours, occasional office days, or a requirement to be based in Singapore. Fully remote employment remains a minority arrangement. Freelance online work is different again: it is self-employment, with its own CPF and tax position.

Most roles described as online still come with a location, a schedule, or both.

Where online work and job scams overlap

Job scams remain among the most commonly reported scam types in Singapore, and many are framed as easy online work. A familiar pattern involves simple tasks — rating products, liking posts, or reviewing hotels — followed by a request to deposit money to "unlock" larger payments. Genuine employers do not ask workers to pay to start a job.

The Singapore Police Force and ScamShield publish current scam alerts, and the ScamShield helpline is 1799.

What genuine online roles commonly state

  • A named employer or client, with a company website and registered business.
  • Working hours or overlap with a team's time zone.
  • Whether the role is employment, a contract, or freelance.
  • Payment terms that do not depend on the worker paying anything first.
