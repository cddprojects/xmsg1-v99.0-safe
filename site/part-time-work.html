Part-time work in Singapore: hours, CPF, and the sectors still hiring

Part-time work has a clear legal definition in Singapore, and in 2026 it is concentrated in the same sectors that are supporting resident employment.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min read

Part-time work is often discussed as a looser form of employment. In Singapore it is not. The Employment Act defines a part-time employee as someone contracted to work fewer than 35 hours a week — and that person is an employee in every other respect.

What counts as part-time

Because part-time staff are employees under a contract of service, the main protections follow from that status. Annual leave and other entitlements are generally pro-rated to the hours worked. For Singapore citizens and permanent residents, CPF applies once total wages exceed $50 a month. Where monthly wages are above $50 and no more than $500, only the employer's share is payable. Contributions fall due by the 14th of the following month, or the next working day.

CPF Board has also announced higher contribution rates for senior employees from 1 January 2027. The current rate tables are published on its website.

A part-time employee is still an employee. That one fact settles most questions about leave and CPF.

Where part-time hours are concentrated

Part-time work is most visible in care, transport and storage, and essential and public services. These are the same sectors the Ministry of Manpower identified as the main sources of resident employment growth in the third quarter of 2026, which makes part-time roles one of the steadier parts of the current market.

In care in particular, part-time positions are often shift-based and attached to a specific centre. In transport and storage, they are usually tied to a site, a shift pattern, and sometimes a licence class.

Part-time work alongside a main job

Some workers hold a part-time role alongside a full-time position. Whether that is possible depends on the hours, rest-day rules, and any outside-work clause in the existing contract. Pay from part-time work depends on the hours actually rostered, which can vary from week to week.

What part-time advertisements commonly state

  • An hourly or monthly rate, and whether there is a minimum number of hours.
  • The shift pattern and how far ahead rosters are published.
  • The site or sites where the work takes place.
  • Whether the role is employment or a freelance engagement, which changes the CPF position.
