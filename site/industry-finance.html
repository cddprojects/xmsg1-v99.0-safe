Finance jobs in Singapore: fewer vacancies, more selective hiring

Financial services recorded one of the two largest vacancy declines by June, and resident employment growth slowed in the third quarter.

By WorkNest SG Editorial19 November 2026 · 2 min readOutlook: Cautious

Work types most often seen: full-time and hybrid

Where the sector stands

Financial services vacancies fell from 5,800 in March 2026 to 4,500 in June, one of the two largest sector declines in the quarter. Retrenchments in the sector rose from 560 in the first quarter to 710 in the second, with business reorganisation the main reason across the economy.

Resident employment growth in financial services was subdued in the third quarter, after resident gains in the second. Compared with 2025, the sector now shows fewer vacancies and leaner teams in some areas, with remaining roles filled more selectively.

Operations, compliance, risk, finance transformation, and client servicing remain the main areas of activity in advertisements.

How roles are usually advertised

Open roles often name a regulation, a product, or a process — reconciliations, case handling, vendor due diligence, management reporting. Hybrid arrangements are widespread, and the number of office days is usually stated. A role described as flexible may still be largely office-based.

Some client-facing and representative roles require a licence. The Monetary Authority of Singapore maintains public registers for those roles, and advertisements often state whether a licence is required or will be sponsored.

Details commonly found in postings

  • The employer of record, including when a role is advertised through an agency.
  • Licence or certification requirements, and who pays for them.
  • Office days and expected availability.
  • The pay structure: base salary, allowances, or commission.

Sources

  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Advance Release, third quarter 2026 (30 October 2026)
  • Ministry of Manpower, Labour Market Report, second quarter 2026 (21 September 2026)
