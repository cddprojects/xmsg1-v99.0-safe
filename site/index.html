The path

Six steps between a job search and an application.

The path is a way to organise the search. It does not submit applications for you, and it does not promise an interview or an offer.

  1. 01

    See where the market is moving

    Separate resident hiring from total employment, and notice which sectors are adding jobs and which are restructuring.

  2. 02

    Read the industry you have in mind

    Each briefing says what the latest figures show, what that means for an application, and what to check in an advertisement.

  3. 03

    Match skills, then look at training

    Start from tasks you have already done. Use public course catalogues and credits before you pay a private provider.

  4. 04

    Choose an arrangement that fits the week

    Full-time, part-time, contract, hybrid, and freelance work are not the same kind of engagement. Pay, CPF, and hours follow from that difference.

  5. 05

    Prepare the application and check the listing

    A short, specific application, sent through a channel you can verify, with a stop list for requests that do not belong in hiring.

  6. 06

    Update the plan when the figures move

    Industry pages carry a review date. The next fuller sector report is expected in mid-December 2026.

How the briefings stay current

General sharing, with a date on it.

Each industry page is a briefing, not a job board. It summarises what the Ministry of Manpower has published, says what an applicant can reasonably take from that, and names the source.

When a new labour market report is released, the affected pages and the update log change together. Until the third-quarter report arrives, vacancy counts on this site are still the June 2026 figures.
